👑 1. Dramatic Size Dimorphism & Giant Taxa
The dataset displays extreme morphological polarization. The largest specimens belong to the genus Gigantometrus (Savandurga KA02 at 146.0mm and KA01 at 139.0mm) and giant desert morphs from Rajasthan (HoSpRJ52 at 123.13mm, RJ58 at 111.23mm), contrasting sharply with miniature buthids such as HoSpTN49 (16.55mm) and HoSpGJ06 (23.59mm).
📐 2. Strong Pedipalp-Body Scaling Allometry
Pedipalp length exhibits strong positive scaling with total body length (r = 0.884). Manus and femur lengths scale proportionally, while movable finger to manus length ratio remains tightly conserved (mean 1.12) across medium buthids, indicating biomechanical chela optimization.
⚖️ 3. Pectinal Tooth Counts & Sexual Dimorphism
Pectinal tooth counts reveal a pronounced bimodal distribution: a female-candidate mode around 21–25 teeth and a male-candidate mode around 31–36 teeth. Bilateral asymmetry (|Left - Right|) is low in 88% of healthy specimens (≤1 tooth difference), while anomalous deviations correlate with flagged injuries.
🗺️ 4. Geographic Clustering & Regional Variance
Specimens from Rajasthan (RJ) have the largest mean total length (70.8 mm), followed by Maharashtra (MH: 55.6 mm), Tamil Nadu (TN: 49.8 mm), and Karnataka (KA: 48.7 mm). Arid/desert habitats show marked gigantism compared to peninsular scrub populations.
